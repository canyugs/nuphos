# Nuphos Trademarks

The code in this repository is Apache-2.0. The name is not.

That is not an extra restriction we are adding — it is what the licence already
says. Apache-2.0 §6:

> This License does not grant permission to use the trade names, trademarks,
> service marks, or product names of the Licensor, except as required for
> describing the origin of the Work and reproducing the content of the NOTICE
> file.

This file exists to say plainly where the line is, because "except as required
for describing the origin of the Work" is doing a lot of work in that sentence
and we would rather you not have to guess.

"Nuphos" and the Nuphos logo are trademarks of Zeabur Inc.

## You do not need to ask us

Use the name to describe things accurately. All of this is fine, and none of it
needs permission:

- "Based on Nuphos", "a fork of Nuphos", "compatible with the Nuphos API".
- "We self-host Nuphos", "we deployed Nuphos on our own cloud".
- Writing about, reviewing, comparing, teaching or criticising Nuphos. A
  negative review is as protected as a positive one.
- Keeping the name where it already appears in source files, `LICENSE`,
  `NOTICE`, commit history and attribution.
- Using the name in a package or repository name that makes the relationship
  clear and subordinate — `nuphos-helm-chart`, `terraform-provider-nuphos`,
  `awesome-nuphos`.

## You do need to ask us

Do not use the name or logo in a way that makes your thing look like our thing:

- Naming your product, service, company, domain or app "Nuphos", or a variation
  close enough to be confused with it.
- Offering a hosted service called Nuphos, or Nuphos-anything, that is not ours.
- Using the Nuphos logo as the icon or mark for your own product.
- Implying endorsement, affiliation, partnership or official status.
- Modifying the logo, or using it in a colour or form we did not publish.

If you distribute a modified build as a product of your own, rename it. You keep
every right Apache-2.0 gives you over the code; the name is how users tell your
build apart from ours, which is the whole point of a trademark.

## Asking

Open a discussion in this repository, or contact Zeabur Inc. We are not looking
for reasons to say no — we read "can I call my thing X" questions as a sign
someone is building something, which is why we published the code.
